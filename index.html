Solving a Real Problem, Step by Step
Tools are easy to learn. Thinking is hard to fake. Here's how I approached a real problem at CUNY — from mess to clarity.
The 26-Institution Reporting Crisis
Real Project · CUNY Research Foundation
1
The Mess
The Research Foundation supports 26 CUNY institutions. Each one tracked workforce and leave data differently — 7+ fragmented data sources, inconsistent column names, mismatched date formats, and reports that took days of manual Excel work. Nobody trusted the numbers. A major reporting deadline was approaching and three institutions had data integrity issues that had gone undetected for two full reporting cycles.
2
The Diagnosis
Before writing any code, I mapped every data source end-to-end: where it originated, who touched it, how it transformed, where it broke. The root cause wasn't bad data — it was no shared schema. Every institution had evolved their own conventions. I also ran Python EDA on the unified layer and that's how I caught the integrity issues nobody knew existed.
3
The Build
I designed a unified GCP reporting layer — consolidated all 7 sources into BigQuery with automated validation rules that flag discrepancies before reports go out, not after. Built ETL pipelines in Python that run on schedule, and created dashboards that operations teams could pull themselves without waiting for an analyst.
4
The Result
Reporting time dropped 55%. Data accuracy improved 37%. Manual prep cut by half. But the real win? Operations teams stopped asking "is this number right?" and started asking "what should we do about it?" That shift — from doubting data to acting on it — is the whole point.
